Security

The Short Version

Life keeps your things safe mostly by keeping them close: on your iPhone, and nowhere of mine.

On Your iPhone

Life's data is stored in the app's own private space on your iPhone, which other apps can't read. When your phone has a passcode, iOS encrypts that space with a key tied to it: after your phone restarts, nothing in it can be read until you unlock the phone.

So the best protection is your own: a passcode, and Face ID or Touch ID.

No Account, No Password

There's nothing to sign in to, so there's no password to steal and no account to break into. I don't run a server that holds anyone's data, so nothing of yours can leak from me.

On the Internet

Every request Life makes uses an encrypted HTTPS connection, checked by iOS. A request carries what the service needs to answer, and nothing from your notes, journal or letters; the Privacy Policy lists each one. Life's questions to the book, show, film and travel services take no cookies, so a service can't use one to tell your phone apart from one question to the next.

Your Photos

A photo you pick for a page is saved as Life's own copy, without the place it was taken. A photo you make a sticker from is not kept at all: only the subject cut out of it, also without the place. Passport and Adventures keep where and when your photos were taken — and, for Passport, what each one shows, worked out on your phone — never the photos themselves; those stay in your library.

Your Lock Screen

With Life Pro, the workout you're doing and, in its last hours, the countdown to an event with an hour show on your Lock Screen. A notification's words can show there too, and, with Life Pro, so do the Life widgets you put there yourself — your goals, what's left today. Turn the first two off in Settings → Notifications in Life, or in your phone's Settings; a widget goes when you touch and hold your Lock Screen and remove it.

Backups

Your iPhone's backups include Life's data. iCloud Backup is encrypted by Apple, and end to end if you turn on Advanced Data Protection. A backup on your computer is safest with Encrypt Local Backup turned on.

If Your Phone Is Lost

Life has no lock of its own: your phone's passcode is what keeps it closed. With Find My, you can erase your iPhone from afar, and Life's data is erased with it.

Found a Problem?

If you find a security problem in Life, please write to me at meftunbusiness@gmail.com. I'll look at it right away and tell you what I did about it.
